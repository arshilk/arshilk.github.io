Device Permissions

The app may request the following device permissions to provide its core functionality:

  • Read Media Audio / Read External Storage: Required to scan your local music library (songs, albums, artists, genres, folders) so the player can list, browse, and play your files. The scan runs entirely on your device; the library index is not transmitted.
  • Modify Audio Settings: Required to apply the equalizer, bass boost, virtualizer, loudness, limiter, noise gate, compressor, and multiband-compression effects to playback. No audio content is captured, recorded, or transmitted.
  • Foreground Service (Media Playback): Used to keep music playing in the background and to expose lock-screen / notification controls via the MediaSession. A persistent notification is shown while a track is loaded.
  • Foreground Service (Special Use - Audio): Used to keep the audio processing engine running so equalizer effects continue to apply during background playback.
  • Internet & Network State: Used to load advertisements (non-premium users), fetch missing lyrics from LrcLib when enabled, deliver crash reports and analytics, and communicate with Google Cast receivers when you start a cast session.
  • Notifications: Used to display the media-playback notification, sleep-timer alerts, and other in-app status updates.
  • Wake Lock: Used to keep audio playback running smoothly while the screen is off.
  • Bluetooth Connect: Used to detect Bluetooth headphone connect / disconnect events so the player can auto-apply your per-device equalizer profile and resume or pause playback as configured.
  • Ignore Battery Optimizations (optional): Optionally requested so the system does not kill the playback service after long periods of background activity. Granting it is optional; declining only affects very-long background sessions.
  • Advertising ID (AD_ID): Used by Google Mobile Ads (AdMob) to serve advertisements to non-premium users. See the dedicated "Advertising and Consent (AdMob & Google UMP)" section below for details on personalized vs. non-personalized ads, the consent form shown in regulated regions, and how to change your choice later.
  • Billing: Used to process in-app purchases (Premium Monthly, Premium Annual, and Premium Lifetime) through Google Play Billing.

Advertising and Consent (AdMob & Google UMP)

Non-premium users see advertisements served by Google AdMob. Premium users (monthly, annual, or lifetime) do not see any ads, and the ad SDK does not request data for them.

To serve ads, AdMob and its partners may process information including your advertising ID (AD_ID), IP address, approximate location derived from IP, device and browser identifiers, app activity (which screens an ad was shown on), and basic device characteristics. The full list of third-party ad partners that may receive this data is published and maintained by Google here: https://support.google.com/admob/answer/9012903.

If you are located in the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom, Switzerland, or a US state with applicable privacy law, on first launch the app shows a consent form managed by Google's User Messaging Platform (UMP). The form asks whether you consent to personalized advertising and to the third-party data processing described above.

  • If you consent, AdMob may serve personalized ads. The legal basis for this processing is your consent.
  • If you decline, ads still appear but are served in a non-personalized "limited ads" mode that does not rely on personal-data profiling. The legal basis for this limited processing is the legitimate interest of supporting a free app, where permitted by local law.
  • If you are outside those regulated regions, no consent form is shown and ads serve in the standard AdMob configuration.

You can change your ad-personalization choice at any time. Inside the app, open Settings → Help & FAQ → EU Privacy Options to re-open Google's consent form and update your selections. This entry is only displayed in regions where the consent form applies. The choice you make there takes effect on subsequent ad requests.

Lyrics (LrcLib)

The app can fetch synced or plain lyrics for tracks that don't already ship with embedded lyrics. When this feature is enabled, the app sends the track title, artist, album, and track duration as query parameters to the public LrcLib API at https://lrclib.net. No advertising ID, account ID, or personally identifying data is sent.

Fetched lyrics are cached on your device so the same track is not re-queried on subsequent plays. You can disable lyrics fetching at any time from Settings → Music Player → Lyrics; when disabled, no network requests are made to LrcLib.

Scrobbling

If you enable Last.fm scrobbling in Settings → Music Player → Integrations, AI Music Player broadcasts now-playing and scrobble events as Android system broadcasts (com.android.music.metachanged / playstatechanged) so that any installed scrobbler app on your device (for example Pano Scrobbler, Simple Scrobbler) can pick them up and submit them to your scrobbling service of choice.

AI Music Player itself does not contact Last.fm, ListenBrainz, or any other scrobbling service directly, and does not store your scrobbling credentials. The broadcasts contain only track metadata (title, artist, album, duration, playback state). Scrobbling is off by default; turning it off immediately stops the broadcasts.

Google Cast / Chromecast

The app integrates the Google Cast SDK so you can stream playback to a Chromecast or other Cast-enabled receiver on your local network. When you tap the Cast button and choose a receiver, track metadata (title, artist, album art) and the audio stream are sent to that receiver through Google's Cast framework. If Google Play Services or the Cast SDK is unavailable on your device, the Cast button is hidden and no Cast-related data is exchanged. Use of the Cast SDK is governed by Google's Cast SDK Additional Developer Terms of Service linked above.

Android Auto / Automotive

When AI Music Player is connected to Android Auto or an Android Automotive head unit, the app exposes its MediaSession (currently playing track, artist, album art, transport controls) to the car system so playback can be controlled from the car's display. No data beyond what is required for media browsing and transport control is exchanged with the car system, and the integration is inactive when the app is not connected to Auto.

Backup and Restore

The app provides a Backup & Restore feature that exports your saved equalizer profiles, player settings, and preferences to a JSON file of your choice on your device or a cloud-storage location you select (such as Google Drive). The app does not upload your backup data to any server controlled by Decibel Studios. Backup files contain only your local app configuration; no personally identifiable information is included.

Headphone EQ Database

The app includes a bundled database of headphone calibration profiles derived from the open-source AutoEq project. This database is shipped with the app and queried entirely on-device; no headphone selection or usage data is transmitted.
