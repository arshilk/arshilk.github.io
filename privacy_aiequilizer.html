Recap Feature and Notification Access

The Recap feature is an optional, opt-in listening summary similar to year-in-review screens you may have seen in other music apps. It shows your top songs, total time listened, liked tracks, and recent playback history across whichever music, video, or podcast apps you use.

To build Recap, the app declares a NotificationListenerService guarded by the BIND_NOTIFICATION_LISTENER_SERVICE system permission. The service is not active until you explicitly grant Notification Access in System Settings → Apps → Special app access → Notification access → AI Equalizer. Until that switch is enabled, no data is captured.

What the listener reads:

  • MediaSession metadata only: song / video / podcast title, artist (or album-artist), album, the package name of the source app (e.g. Spotify, YouTube), playback state (playing/paused), and the album-art bitmap published by that app.
  • Album-art bitmaps are scaled down to a maximum of 128 pixels on the longest edge before being held in memory, so a thumbnail can be shown in the now-playing chip.

What the listener does not read:

  • No message contents, email previews, chat notifications, banking alerts, calendar reminders, or any other non-media notification.
  • No notification text from apps that are not currently posting a MediaSession (the listener filters strictly to active media sessions and ignores everything else).
  • No contacts, no clipboard, no location, no microphone, no screen content.

Where the data lives: Recap stores playback events in a private JSON file (recap.json) inside the app's own internal storage on your device. Nothing from Recap is uploaded to Decibel Studios, Google, or any third party. The data never leaves your device.

How to revoke: Open System Settings → Apps → Special app access → Notification access and toggle AI Equalizer off. The listener is immediately unbound and stops capturing new events. Previously-stored events remain on the device until you clear them or uninstall the app.

How to clear what was already captured: Inside the app, open the Recap screen and tap the trash (Clear History) icon in the top bar. This deletes the entire local Recap database. Uninstalling AI Equalizer also deletes the Recap database along with all other app data.
